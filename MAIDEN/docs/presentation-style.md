# MAIDEN status presentations — style guide

Condensed from an executive-communication talk (Aug 2026), kept because it
matches how MAIDEN reviews are judged: leadership reads judgment and
clarity, not effort. Every status presentation is an audition for
sponsorship of the next phase.

## The one rule

Open with the outcome and the recommendation. Background only if asked.
Never "let me walk you through what we did" — always "here's where we
stand, the risk, and what I recommend."

## Structure (5 slides, one idea each, headline = conclusion)

1. **Hook** — impact + urgency in one sentence.
   *MAIDEN-shaped example:* "The FFT that fit no ECP5 now uses 4% of one
   board — capacity is closed, and here is what that frees."
2. **Tension** — the 2–3 insights that matter, already filtered. Filtering
   is the leadership signal; the other twenty findings go to backup slides.
3. **Climax** — Action → Reason → Benefit. "If we do X, we get Y at cost Z."
   Ownership language: "here's what we should do," not "here's what we found."
4. **Resolution** — next step with owner and date. Never "that's my
   update"; always "if aligned, we do X by DATE and review at MILESTONE."
5. **Call to action** — 2–3 options with the risk of each and one explicit
   recommendation. Make the yes easy.

## Data discipline

- A number earns a slide only if it changes a decision (revenue, cost,
  risk, schedule). MAIDEN's measured figures are its strength — use the
  ones that decide something (e.g., "9.8% of the board" decides the
  buy-nothing question), not the full table. Tables go to backup.
- Tie every data point to risk, opportunity, or decision — otherwise it
  reads as reporting, not leading.

## Delivery

- Short sentences, conclusions first, no emotional padding, comfort with
  pauses. Talk to the leaders, not the slides.
- If the meeting ends and nothing changes, the presentation failed.

## The five silent disqualifiers

1. Opening with agenda/background. 2. Decorative overloaded decks.
3. No clear recommendation. 4. Talking to slides. 5. Data without insight.

## Applied to MAIDEN artifacts

- Whitepaper/briefing updates: exec summary carries the ask and the
  materials number up front (already the pattern).
- Milestone reviews (maiden36 etc.): open with the gate decision to be
  made, present the measured evidence for it, end with the order/no-order
  or proceed/hold recommendation and its date.
